# Whiskey Decanter Sets Wholesale: Components, Stoppers & Gift Boxes

A whiskey decanter set is a group of items sold together. The bottle, stopper, drinking glasses, display base and packaging each need a clear description. Before comparing prices, establish what one quoted set contains and which sample represents the finished order.

Start with the [whiskey decanter set collection](/products/category/whiskey-decanter-set) and the [Wine, Spirits & Gift Sets guide](/guides/wine-spirits-gifting). This checklist focuses on set composition, closure fit and packed-sample approval. It does not establish specifications or performance for an individual product.

## 1. Write a component list for each shortlisted set

Use the item reference as the starting point, then list the contents individually. A title such as “decanter set” does not describe whether the quotation includes drinking glasses, a base or a retail box. A product photograph may also show display items that are outside the order.

- Decanter: item reference, quantity, finish and capacity with its stated measurement convention.
- Stopper: quantity, material, seating design and any separate sealing component.
- Drinking glasses: item reference, quantity, capacity and finish for each style.
- Base or stand: material, quantity and the items it is intended to hold.
- Other accessories: a separate description and quantity for each included item.
- Packing: individual protection, retail box, insert and transport carton.

Keep optional accessories separate from included components. If two quotations contain different items, compare the glassware and packing lines individually before comparing the final set price.

## 2. Read capacities and materials by component

A combined capacity field is not a description of every item. Record the decanter and each drinking-glass style on separate lines, with units. Distinguish the supplier's nominal size, the agreed brimful measurement and the intended serving volume. Refer to the [glassware specification-sheet guide](/blog/glassware-spec-sheet-guide-wholesale) when the measurement convention is missing.

The same approach applies to material. The decanter, glasses, stopper, seal and base may use different materials. Keep their descriptions separate rather than applying one material name to the complete set. Decoration and manufacturing descriptions also belong in their own fields.

During sample review, record the measurement source and the approved specification revision. Do not use outside dimensions to calculate the internal volume of an irregular bottle.

## 3. Review the stopper with the decanter

Handle the stopper and bottle as one assembly. Check the seating surfaces, insertion and removal, the way the stopper rests, and any instructions supplied for the closure. Record observations against the exact sample reference.

A decorative stopper does not establish an airtight storage seal. If sealed storage is part of the buying brief, describe that requirement separately and request evidence for the proposed assembly and use conditions. Keep any supplier instruction with the approved sample record.

Consider how the closure is packed. A stopper left loose inside the gift box can contact the bottle or glasses during handling. Review its restraint and separation in the assembled package. Record a replacement-part reference where the supplier provides one; similar appearance alone does not establish interchangeability.

## 4. Assess the bottle, glasses and base together

Place the complete sample on the surface where it will be displayed or used. Review access to the bottle and glasses, the available space around the set and the handling of individual items. Where a stand is included, check its seating points and the intended loading arrangement.

For the glassware, review rims, bases, visible finish and the pouring experience using the agreed sample method. Record decoration placement and orientation separately for the decanter and glasses. A design approved on a bottle should not automatically become the approved design on every glass.

Keep care instructions specific to the finished components. Washing instructions for undecorated glass do not establish suitability for a metallic finish, printed design, wooden base or removable seal.

## 5. Approve the gift box and transport carton separately

Review a sample packed with all the intended components. The insert should match the actual shapes and arrangement. Observe movement, contact between parts, protection around rims and the stopper, and the space needed to remove each item.

The retail box presents the set to the customer; the transport carton serves a different handling role. Include both in the packing brief. Record the number of sets per carton, packed dimensions and gross weight from the supplier's agreed packing sheet. Do not transfer carton figures from a different set or packing option.

If the project has a transport-test requirement, define the packed configuration, test method and acceptance criteria with the responsible parties. An attractive gift box or a sample fit check does not demonstrate transport performance. The [glassware packaging guide](/blog/shot-glass-packaging-bulk-dividers-gift-boxes-carton-checks) explains the distinction between retail packing and outer-carton protection.

## 6. Keep pieces, sets and cartons clear in the quotation

Use a separate unit for each commercial line. State whether the price is per complete set, per decanter, per glass or per packing component. Show any decoration, tooling or packing charge as its own line when quoted that way by the supplier.

Record the composition behind every “set” quantity. Keep the set count, the number of each component and the carton count on the same order record so that receiving staff can reconcile the delivery. Use the agreed carton configuration for transport planning.

Request the relevant order quantity, destination and required timing in the same brief. Minimum quantities and delivery terms belong to the specific quotation; a quantity listed for another model or packing option should not become a promise for this set.

For retail projects, keep box artwork, labels and barcode requirements in the approval file. The [private-label packaging checklist](/blog/private-label-glassware-packaging-barcodes) provides a separate workflow for those items.

## 7. Retain one complete approval record

Download the [decanter-set checklist (Excel)](https://media.glarivoglass.com/site/blog/whiskey-decanter-sets-wholesale-components-stoppers-gift-boxes/whiskey-decanter-set-checklist-de79786115cb.xlsx) to record components, packing requirements and quotation units for the selected sample revision.

Approve a specific sample and specification revision. The record should identify all glassware, closures, bases and packing components, together with decoration artwork and the accepted arrangement inside the box.

Photograph the assembled set and the opened package. Retain the approved sample where practical. Record any exclusions or changes before issuing the purchase order. A bare decanter sample can help shortlist a bottle, but it does not approve glasses, a stopper or the finished retail pack.

For a repeat order, use the retained record to compare component references, finish, closure, artwork and packing. Review any proposed substitution before production. The same model name is not a complete description of the approved set.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the product picture define what is included?

Use the written component list and quotation. Review the picture against that list, and record display props or optional items separately.

### Does a stopper make a decanter suitable for sealed storage?

The closure needs its own specification and supporting evidence for the intended use. Its appearance does not establish a storage seal.

### Can I approve the bottle first and order the complete set later?

Bottle approval is one stage of the process. Review the glasses, closure, base, decoration and packed assembly before approving the complete set.
