
A custom shot glass order can look straightforward until the first decorated sample arrives. The logo may be sharp but face the wrong direction, sit lower than expected, or look different against clear glass than it did on a white screen. A gift-box insert can also touch an area that the buyer intended to keep visible and unmarked.

The most useful approval package connects four things: **the exact glass, the artwork revision, the decorated physical sample and the packing specification**. This checklist helps bar buyers, souvenir retailers and promotional distributors prepare that package before releasing an order.


## Quick checklist: what to approve before production

- The selected glass reference, dimensions and intended use.
- The decoration process proposed for that glass and artwork.
- Final artwork files, physical size, colors and placement references.
- A digital proof identified by product and artwork revision.
- A decorated physical sample representing the intended finish.
- Written care instructions and any required supporting evidence for the finished item.
- Packing, quantities per design, charges and the agreed production-release conditions.

An electronic proof helps confirm the design layout. A physical sample helps confirm its appearance on glass. Neither replaces an agreed inspection plan for the production order.

For help selecting the glass itself, begin with the [Shot Glass Buying Guide for Wholesale Buyers](https://www.glarivoglass.com/blog/shot-glass-buying-guide-for-wholesale-buyers). This article focuses on the artwork and approval stage.

## 1. Define the customization scope before sending a logo

Start by distinguishing decoration on an existing glass from development of a new shape. Those are different projects. For an existing model, ask which processes are available on that particular surface and whether any material, coating or shape features limit the decoration.

The appearance you want should guide the discussion: a simple mark, fine lettering, a multicolor illustration or a frosted effect. Ask the supplier to identify the proposed process and explain how it handles the smallest details in your design. Do not assume that two similar-looking samples use the same process or have the same care requirements.

Specify the quantity **per design and per colorway**, not only the combined order total. Confirm minimum quantities, setup costs, sample charges and timing against that exact scope. Record whether a quoted timeline begins after payment, artwork approval, physical-sample approval or another agreed milestone.

## 2. Prepare an artwork brief the supplier can use

Send the original artwork where available and a viewable reference showing the intended result. Ask the decorator which production file formats it accepts. An AI, EPS or PDF file can contain embedded raster images, so a filename alone does not prove that the design is vector artwork.

Vector shapes can be resized without the pixel limitations of a bitmap; photographs and other raster content need suitable resolution at the intended output size. Adobe explains the distinction in its [guide to raster and vector images](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/using/image-essentials.html). Have the decorator assess the actual file at the proposed print size rather than relying on a universal resolution rule.

Keep an editable master. For the production copy, ask whether fonts should be outlined or embedded, and how linked images should be supplied. Check the exported proof for substituted characters, missing symbols and changes to spacing.

Use this brief to keep the order details together:

- **Glass reference:** Selected model, shape and drawing or sample reference
- **Design identity:** Design name, revision and file name
- **Artwork files:** Original production artwork plus a viewable reference
- **Finished artwork size:** Width and height with units; confirm how curved or wraparound artwork is dimensioned
- **Placement:** Front/back orientation and distances from agreed reference points
- **Color and finish:** Color references, desired opacity and surface appearance
- **Detail requirements:** Smallest lettering, fine lines, gaps and any details that must remain readable
- **Order split:** Quantity per design/colorway and packing configuration
- **Use and destination:** Intended channel, cleaning conditions and destination country

For a souvenir collection, give every design its own identifier. For a two-sided logo, label each side and define their relationship. The phrase “logo on both sides” leaves too much room for interpretation.

## 3. Define the printable area on the selected glass

A logo that fits inside a photograph may not fit the decorator's usable print area. Ask for a template or dimensioned proof for the selected glass. Confirm which areas must remain clear and how the design relates to seams, handles, curves and the rim.

Agree the measurement reference points. For example, specify whether the vertical position is measured from the glass base to the bottom of the artwork or to its center. For tapered glasses, clarify whether artwork width means a flat artwork dimension, a distance around the surface or a front-view projection. These are different measurements.

![AI-generated illustration comparing a flat artwork proof with a decorated shot glass. Placement marks are schematic, not a dimensioned production template.](/images/blog/custom-logo-shot-glasses-artwork-sample-approval-checklist/artwork-placement.webp)

For a wraparound design, confirm where it starts and ends, whether a gap is expected and how any joint should appear. For a two-sided design, ask for front and back views with an orientation reference. Avoid using “centered” as the only placement instruction.

Agree measurable acceptance limits with the decorator for the selected process. This guide does not prescribe a universal print tolerance or rim clearance. Those values need to come from the actual product, process and applicable requirements.

## 4. Review the digital proof at the intended size

The digital proof should identify the glass, artwork revision, proposed process and the dimensions used for approval. Check every design in a mixed order; approving one logo does not approve all other colorways or versions.

Review these points before requesting the decorated sample:

1. **Content:** spelling, punctuation, dates, symbols and orientation match the final artwork.
2. **Scale:** width and height match the brief, and small text is readable at the intended physical size.
3. **Placement:** front/back views, measurement points and any seam or handle relationship are clear.
4. **Color instructions:** references and desired opacity are written down; any proposed underbase or additional layer is identified.
5. **Unresolved details:** substitutions, unavailable effects and process limitations are listed for a decision.

A paper print at actual size can help you judge the layout. Confirm that the printer has not scaled it to fit the page. Paper and a monitor still cannot reproduce the complete appearance of decoration on curved, transparent glass.

If fine text or a narrow gap is difficult to reproduce, agree a revised design and issue a new proof. Keep the superseded version clearly marked so that the factory does not receive competing approvals.

## 5. Evaluate the decorated physical sample

Ask what the sample represents: the intended glass blank, decoration materials, process and packing, or a substitute used only to demonstrate part of the design. Record any difference from planned production before treating the sample as a reference.

Compare the sample with the final brief and proof. View it from the intended front and back, rotate it, and inspect it against both light and dark backgrounds. If presentation with a drink matters, include the intended serving appearance in the review. Use agreed lighting and a physical color reference where color is critical; a phone photograph alone is not a dependable color master.

- **Sample identity:** Glass reference, sample ID, artwork revision and process
- **Glass body:** Dimensions, capacity definition, rim condition and base stability against the agreed specification
- **Artwork position:** Actual width, height and position using the agreed reference points
- **Graphic detail:** Legibility, edges, gaps, line detail and alignment between colors
- **Appearance:** Color, opacity, finish and visibility from the intended viewing angles
- **Surface condition:** Visible scratches, smears, incomplete areas or other departures from the agreed criteria
- **Care and performance:** Instructions and relevant test evidence identifying the finished decoration
- **Packing contact:** Areas touching the insert, wrap or adjacent glass; visible movement or rubbing

An attractive sample does not establish wash durability or suitability for a particular use. If dishwasher performance, abrasion resistance or another property matters, agree the test method, conditions, acceptance criteria and evidence with the supplier before approval. Instructions for an undecorated blank should not automatically be applied to its decorated version.

Visual checks also do not replace relevant product documentation. Keep document review as a separate item in the order approval process.

## 6. Check the packed sample as well as the glass

Review the individual pack, any inner pack and the master-carton specification separately. Confirm that the intended decorated face is presented correctly and that the insert fits the actual glass, including handles or other projections.

![AI-generated illustration of a decorated shot glass, a packed sample and an inspection notepad. Conceptual scene, not a record of an actual inspection or transport test.](/images/blog/custom-logo-shot-glasses-artwork-sample-approval-checklist/sample-review.webp)

Identify where the packaging contacts the decoration and whether glasses can touch each other. Ask for the actual packing materials and configuration to be included in the sample review. A neat sample box does not demonstrate how the bulk shipment will perform; agree any required transport validation for the final packed configuration separately.

For retail labels, barcodes and broader private-label planning, use the [Private Label Glassware: Packaging, Barcodes & Retail Readiness guide](https://www.glarivoglass.com/blog/private-label-glassware-packaging-barcodes).

## 7. Sign off a specific version and define change control

Keep a written approval record linked to the sample and artwork. The buyer and supplier should retain matching references, supported by photographs and the same specification revision.

Copy this form into your approval record:

- **Buyer / project / order reference:** ______________________________
- **Glass model and specification revision:** ______________________________
- **Design name and artwork revision:** ______________________________
- **Sample ID and date received:** ______________________________
- **Confirmed decoration process and color reference:** ______________________________
- **Approved artwork size, position and agreed limits:** ______________________________
- **Packing specification and sample reference:** ______________________________
- **Care instructions / supporting document references:** ______________________________
- **Open issues and required corrections:** ______________________________
- **Decision:** Approved / Revise and resubmit / Rejected
- **Approved scope and exclusions:** ______________________________
- **Production release:** Hold / Released by named approver
- **Approver, date and retained-reference location:** ______________________________

Separate approval of artwork from permission to begin production. If packing or a sample correction remains unresolved, record the hold explicitly. A brief email saying “looks good” can leave the approved scope unclear.

Require written review of later changes to the glass blank, artwork, decoration materials, process or packing. Decide whether the change needs a revised proof, another sample or additional verification. For repeat orders, reference the approved version and reconfirm current availability and commercial terms.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I send a PNG or a screenshot of my logo?

You can send it as an initial visual reference. Whether it is suitable for production depends on its detail, resolution at the finished size and the decoration process. Supply the original artwork where possible and ask the decorator to confirm or redraw it for your approval.

### Is an approved digital mockup enough to approve the order?

It can confirm the design layout within its stated scope. If color, opacity, fine detail, curved placement or packaging fit matters, review a decorated physical sample as well. Keep performance and document requirements separate from appearance approval.

### What is the minimum order for custom logo shot glasses?

There is no single minimum that this guide can promise. Ask for the requirement for the exact model, process, design, colorway and packing format, including how a mixed-design order is counted.

### Will the logo match the color on my screen?

Use the screen as a visual reference, then agree a color target and review the physical finish under stated conditions. The glass, decoration, background and lighting affect how the result is seen. Record the approved reference and any agreed limits.

### Can decorated shot glasses go in a commercial dishwasher?

Request written care instructions for the exact finished product and evidence relevant to your cleaning conditions when this is a purchase requirement. Neither a clear glass blank nor an attractive logo proves suitability for a commercial wash cycle.

### What if I change the logo after approving the sample?

Issue a new artwork revision, identify what changed and confirm whether production is on hold. Ask the supplier to reconfirm proofing, sample requirements, charges and timing before releasing the revised order.
