Private label glassware needs a coordinated product, packaging and data specification. A clear tumbler can be supplied in several different retail configurations, and each configuration needs to match the buyer's order, artwork, product records and handling requirements.

Start with the sales channel and the unit being sold: one glass, a matched pair, an assorted set or a gift pack. Then agree the packaging structure, identification, label text and approval steps before releasing bulk production. This guide provides a purchasing workflow; exact legal and retailer requirements must be confirmed for the intended destination and product.

## 1. Define what the private-label order includes

Private label means the product is sold under the commissioning business's brand. It does not automatically mean an individual printed box, three packaging layers or a higher minimum order quantity. A sleeve, label, display tray or gift box may be appropriate, depending on the brief.

The main change is the number of specifications that must stay aligned. Record the glass, decoration, retail unit, pack artwork, identifiers and shipping configuration as one controlled order. Unbranded goods still require the applicable safety and regulatory assessment; adding a brand does not create a separate “full compliance” tier.

- **Glass specification:** product reference, dimensions, capacity convention, finish and approved sample.
- **Retail unit:** contents, item count, variants and any included accessories.
- **Packaging:** construction, inserts, materials, appearance and distribution route.
- **Product data:** brand, description, identifiers, dimensions and pack relationships.
- **Approval responsibility:** who signs off artwork, identification, label wording, testing and shipment release.

## 2. Specify the packaging levels that the order actually needs

### Consumer or retail pack

This is the unit presented to the customer: for example, a single-glass carton, a sleeve or a boxed set. Specify how the glass is retained, how the pack opens, its finished dimensions and which panel carries the required information. Confirm whether the buyer wants boxed sale, open display or another format.

### Inner pack, where used

An inner tray or box can group retail units for handling and protection. Confirm its quantity, dividers and opening method with the retailer. Inner-pack and shelf-ready tray requirements may be part of the buyer's specification; they are not always optional supplier decisions.

### Shipping carton and load configuration

Document units per carton, internal protection, external dimensions, packed weight, sealing method and case labels. Where pallet delivery is involved, confirm stacking and load requirements. A retail carton that looks good on a shelf still needs a suitable transport system.

## 3. Separate the product identifier from the barcode artwork

### Who controls the number?

A GTIN identifies a trade item. A barcode is a machine-readable carrier of data, while the retailer's product file supplies information such as the description and price. Correct artwork cannot compensate for a mismatched product record.

GS1 states that the brand owner is normally responsible for assigning the GTIN, regardless of where the item is manufactured. For a private-label line, this may be the retailer, importer or other commissioning brand owner. A supplier can help produce artwork using an authorized number; generating the image does not itself transfer responsibility for the identifier. [GS1: responsibility for numbering trade items](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734414/).

Agree the responsible organization and obtain identifiers through the appropriate GS1 Member Organisation. Do not assume that a third-party barcode purchase provides a company prefix licensed to your brand, and do not invent or borrow identifiers from another product.

### Choose the carrier for the scanning environment

UPC-A carries a GTIN-12 and EAN-13 carries a GTIN-13. Both are used internationally; the destination alone does not decide which one a retailer requires. Confirm compatibility with the buyer's systems. [GS1: international EAN/UPC acceptance](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734150-are-ean-13-upc-a-ean-8-and-upc-e-symbols-fully-accepted-worldwide-).

Case identification may use ITF-14 or GS1-128 depending on the application. A logistics unit may instead need an SSCC, which identifies that logistics unit rather than replacing the trade-item GTIN. Obtain the retailer's case and logistics label specification before preparing artwork. [GS1: logistic label guideline](https://www.gs1.org/standards/gs1-logistic-label-guideline/1-3).

### Keep product and pack changes under control

Apply the GS1 GTIN Management Standard to new items and changes. Different retail configurations may need separate identifiers; do not decide solely from a warehouse SKU name. A change in trade-item quantity within a case requires a new GTIN at that affected case level, without necessarily changing the unchanged retail unit's GTIN. [GS1: pack/case quantity rule](https://www.gs1.org/1/gtinrules/en/rule/270/packcase-quantity).

Keep a mapping of the consumer unit, case contents and any higher trade-item levels used by the buyer. When changing a glass colour, brand or pack configuration, review the relevant allocation rule before printing.

### Verify the finished printed symbol

The print brief should name the authorized data, symbology, target dimensions, placement, quiet zones, substrate and required quality criterion. Avoid folds, seams, obstructing labels and poorly chosen curved or reflective areas. Have the printer confirm the design against the intended scanning application. [GS1: barcode quality checks](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734141-what-should-i-check-to-ensure-good-quality-barcodes-).

Use final printed samples for verification. A successful scan with one handheld scanner is a useful functional check, but it does not replace formal barcode verification of symbol quality and encoded data. Request the verification report against the agreed specification. [GS1: barcode verification guidelines](https://ref.gs1.org/guidelines/barcode-verification/).

## 4. Build a destination-specific label brief

Separate legally required information, retailer-required information and voluntary marketing claims. Review the actual product and pack rather than using the same generic panel for every market.

### Country of origin

For US imports, the marking method needs an applicability review. US rules expressly allow paper or pressure-sensitive labels when conspicuous and securely attached so they remain until the ultimate purchaser receives the article, unless deliberately removed. A blanket “removable stickers are prohibited” instruction is therefore inaccurate. Confirm any special rules and whether container marking is acceptable for the actual presentation. [19 CFR 134.44: acceptable marking methods](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-134/subpart-E/section-134.44).

Use the substantiated country of origin. Do not assume that adding artwork or retail packaging changes the origin, or that a printed outer carton always removes the need to mark an individual glass.

### Responsible parties and safety information

For relevant EU consumer products, review the General Product Safety Regulation alongside product-specific rules. The required information can include product identification, manufacturer and relevant EU economic-operator details, postal and electronic contact information, and applicable warnings or instructions. Placement and language depend on the obligation and product circumstances; online offers also need review. [European Commission: GPSR guidance](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52025XC06233).

Also check destination-specific packaging, recycling and producer-responsibility requirements with the responsible importer. A supplier's standard label is not a substitute for that review.

### Material, capacity and care claims

Use verified specifications. Glass composition and treatment are different attributes: “tempered” describes a treatment, so it should not simply replace the material description. If a capacity is stated, clarify whether it is brimful capacity or the intended serving volume. Measured-service claims may require a separate local assessment.

Confirm care instructions for the complete supplied article, including decoration, lids and accessories. Do not turn a glass-body statement into a dishwasher or thermal-use claim for the finished set. Keep qualifiers and translated instructions consistent with the supporting evidence.

### Food-contact information

EU food-contact legislation has its own safety, labelling and traceability provisions. Do not reduce these to a universal requirement to print “FDA / LFGB / Prop 65 compliant” on the box. [European Commission: food-contact legislation](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/chemical-safety/food-contact-materials/legislation_en).

Keep the relevant product evidence in the compliance file and establish any warning requirement separately. For a fuller review, use the [glassware food-contact safety guide](/blog/glassware-food-contact-safety-fda-lfgb-prop-65).

## 5. Agree retail and transport acceptance criteria

Ask the buyer for written requirements before designing around an assumed universal checklist. Relevant questions include how the pack is displayed, where it is scanned, how staff replenish it and how it moves through the distribution network.

- Will the glass be sold boxed, displayed loose or presented in a tray?
- What shelf footprint, orientation and opening method are required?
- Are inner quantities, case quantities or pallet patterns prescribed?
- Which product data fields and images must be supplied for onboarding?
- What visual, stability and handling checks will the buyer apply?
- Which transport test and acceptance criteria fit the actual route?

Assess the complete packaged product for the relevant distribution conditions. Parcel delivery, mixed-load distribution and pallet transport can require different test approaches. ISTA provides procedures and selection guidance for different packaged-product and distribution situations; agree the appropriate approach with the buyer and laboratory. A simple shelf bump is not a substitute for transport testing. [ISTA: test procedures](https://www.ista.org/test_procedures.php).

## 6. Compare quotations by cost item and minimum quantity

Request an itemized quote for the defined configuration. There is no dependable universal percentage split between glass, printed boxes and freight: construction, volume, transport route and delivery terms can change the balance.

- **Glass and decoration:** unit price, tooling or setup charges, colour or artwork minimums.
- **Retail pack:** structure, board or other material, printing, finishing and inserts.
- **Inner and shipping packs:** quantities, dividers, protective materials and case labels.
- **Packing labor:** assembly, labelling, sorting and any special inspection.
- **Development and validation:** prototypes, print proofs, barcode verification and agreed testing.
- **Delivery basis:** named shipping terms, freight scope and which destination charges are included or excluded.
- **Reorder conditions:** repeat quantities, retained tooling, unused printed stock and storage responsibility.

Ask for the MOQ of each relevant component. Glass, decoration, printing or inserts may be the constraint; it is not always the box. Confirm whether quantities can be shared across variants and who pays for surplus packaging. Evaluate print methods and common structures with actual quotations rather than assuming a particular run size is uneconomic.

Build the schedule from the dependent approvals: product selection, structural sample, identifiers and copy, artwork, physical proof, validation, bulk production, packing and shipment. The longest dependency may be glass, decoration or packaging. Ask each supplier for a dated plan and identify what starts only after approval.

## 7. Use a launch approval checklist

Before authorizing bulk production and later shipment release, complete the applicable checks and assign an owner for each open item.

1. Confirm the glass reference, finished specification and retail set contents.
2. Document the brand owner's identifier responsibility and approved GTIN-to-product mapping.
3. Align consumer, case and logistics data with the buyer's requirements.
4. Approve pack structure using the actual glass and intended materials.
5. Review label content, claims, language, origin marking and responsible-party details for the destination.
6. Approve a physical print proof on the intended substrate and finish; record the artwork revision.
7. Verify the barcode on finished printed material and confirm the decoded data matches the product file.
8. Complete the agreed display, handling and transport checks using representative packed samples.
9. Lock pack quantities, dimensions and weights in the order and buyer data file.
10. Record sample approval, release responsibility, change notification rules and treatment of nonconforming goods.

Keep dated approval records. If a component or artwork changes after approval, assess the effect on identification, claims, fit and testing before accepting the substitution.

## 8. Four avoidable launch mistakes

### Confusing barcode artwork with identifier control

A printer can generate a symbol from authorized data. Confirm who assigns and maintains the identifier, then keep the same approved mapping across factories and artwork revisions where the allocation rules allow it.

### Comparing quotes for different pack configurations

A lower price may exclude inserts, assembly, proofs or testing. Compare the same glass, unit contents, packaging specification and delivery scope before treating a quote as cheaper.

### Approving only the screen artwork

Check a physical proof for colour, folds, text legibility and symbol placement. Keep final printed-sample verification as a separate step, since a good-looking proof does not establish barcode quality.

### Releasing production while decisions remain open

Unconfirmed label copy or case data can hold up the whole order. Use an approval schedule with named owners and document any change to the agreed specification.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does private-label glassware always need a custom gift box?

No. Choose the pack around the buyer's display, protection and information requirements. A simpler format may be suitable when it meets those requirements.

### Can the factory help with barcodes?

Yes, it can help prepare and print artwork using authorized data. The commissioning business should still establish the correct allocation responsibility and maintain its product records.

### Can one packaging design be shared across several glasses?

Potentially, but validate each combination. Check the insert, movement, finished appearance, weight and identification for each covered variant.

### Is a readable barcode enough to approve a launch?

It addresses only part of the job. The identifier, product file, label text, pack configuration and agreed validation must also match the approved order.
